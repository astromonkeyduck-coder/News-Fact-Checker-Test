Our standards

Editorial policy.

These are the rules the Noteworthy News newsroom operates under. They are not aspirations; they are constraints. Where a story and this policy conflict, the policy wins.

Accuracy first

Accuracy outranks speed, traffic, and everything else. We publish when we can stand behind a claim, not when a claim starts trending. The mechanics of that commitment - source rules, media checks, and the developing/confirmed distinction - are documented in How we verify, which is part of this policy.

No update is published as fact unless it traces to primary documentation, an official source with direct knowledge, or two independent sources. Anything short of that bar is labeled developing and attributed.

Independence

Noteworthy News is independently operated. No advertiser, platform, source, or outside party reviews, approves, or influences coverage before publication.

  • We do not accept payment, gifts, or favors in exchange for coverage or for killing a story.
  • Sources do not get copy approval or preview access to stories about them.
  • Coverage decisions are made on news value alone: significance, verifiability, and public interest.

Sourcing and attribution

Readers should be able to see where a claim comes from without leaving the update. In practice:

  • Every update carries a source note. "According to" names a specific source, not a vague cloud of reports.
  • When we build on another outlet's reporting, we credit and link the outlet by name.
  • When we cite data, we identify the dataset and its limitations, and link the original where possible.
  • Quotes are not altered beyond trimming for length, and trims never change meaning.

Anonymous sources

Anonymity is a tool for protecting people, not for laundering weak claims. We grant it only when all three conditions hold:

  1. The information is significant and cannot reasonably be obtained on the record.
  2. The source has direct knowledge, and we know their identity and motivation even if readers do not.
  3. Naming the source would expose them to genuine risk - legal, professional, or physical.

When we use an anonymous source, we describe their position as precisely as safety allows, and we corroborate their account before publishing. A commitment to protect a source, once made, is kept.

Fairness and right of reply

Anyone who is the subject of a significant claim in our reporting gets a genuine opportunity to respond before publication where practical, and a prominent one after publication where not. Fairness also means:

  • We represent opposing positions accurately, in terms their holders would recognize.
  • We do not manufacture balance where the evidence is one-sided; fairness is owed to people, not to false claims.
  • Headlines and alerts must be supported by the story beneath them. No bait.

News, analysis, and opinion

Three kinds of writing appear on Noteworthy News, and each is labeled:

  • News Verified reporting on what happened. No advocacy, no speculation presented as fact.
  • Analysis Interpretation grounded in the reporting: why an event matters, what may follow. Labeled as analysis and kept out of the factual timeline.
  • Opinion Argued viewpoints, clearly attributed to their author. Rare on this site, and never blended into coverage.

Corrections

Errors are corrected quickly, visibly, and in the same place the error appeared. The original claim stays in the record with the correction attached; we do not silently rewrite history. Material errors are flagged at the top of the affected story, and corrected alerts follow erroneous alerts through the same channel. Details are in How we verify.

Conflicts of interest

Anyone reporting for Noteworthy News must disclose financial holdings, personal relationships, or affiliations that intersect with a story. Where a conflict exists, the person steps away from that coverage or the conflict is disclosed to readers inside the story - whichever protects readers better. Staff do not trade on nonpublic information learned through reporting.

Advertising and funding

The site is currently supported by advertising served through Google AdSense. That relationship has no influence on coverage:

  • Ads are visually distinct from editorial content and never dressed up as news.
  • Advertisers receive no preview, veto, or coverage consideration of any kind.
  • If we ever publish sponsored or partner content, it will be labeled unmistakably as such.

AI in our newsroom

We build with AI - including the Noteworthy News AI assistant on this site - and we use it under strict rules:

  • AI tools may assist with research, monitoring, and drafting, but a human is responsible for every published update and every fact in it.
  • AI is never the source of a factual claim. Verification always traces to real sources under the rules above.
  • Our AI assistant is grounded in our reporting, cites its sources, and says plainly when something is not confirmed.
  • We do not publish AI-generated imagery presented as real photography or footage of events.

Minimizing harm

Reporting the news can hurt people. We weigh public interest against private harm in every case, and specifically:

  • We follow verified casualty and missing-person information from authorities rather than amplifying rumor during disasters.
  • We avoid graphic material unless it is essential to understanding the story, and we warn readers when it is.
  • We do not identify minors, victims of certain crimes, or private individuals caught up in news events unless the public interest clearly requires it.
  • We cover suicide and mass violence in line with established safe-reporting guidance, avoiding method detail and sensational framing.

Enforcement

A policy that is not enforced is decoration. Violations of this policy are treated as serious editorial failures: coverage is corrected in public, and the process failure is fixed so it does not repeat. If you believe our reporting has fallen short of anything on this page, contact us - correction requests are read first.

Questions about this policy?

We answer questions about our standards directly, and we revise this document when our practices change.
