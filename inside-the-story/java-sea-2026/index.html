01 / The event

One event, revised estimates.

The USGS record places this earthquake in the Java Sea at Sep 11, 2026, 09:23:55 PM UTC. Its later saved solution reports magnitude 6.5 and a depth of 372 kilometres.

An earlier preserved origin solution reports magnitude 6.6. The later 6.5 estimate belongs to the same event. A revised estimate changes the scientific account; it does not mean the earthquake itself happened again.

02 / Reading the shaking

One magnitude, different intensities.

Magnitude describes the size of an earthquake at its source. Intensity describes shaking at a location, so it can differ from place to place. The map’s MMI values describe the agency’s estimated shaking, not a second magnitude.

USGS: magnitude and intensity ↗

This feature uses the supplied ShakeMap grid. The displayed low-resolution grid spans MMI 2.8–2.9. This is the range in this supplied grid, not a claim about the maximum shaking everywhere. Its cells are estimates, not individual sensor readings or reports of damage. Areas outside that grid are outside the display’s coverage; they are not evidence of no shaking.

03 / The regional record

Context without a causal claim.

The regional view uses a fixed geographic area, date window and minimum magnitude. Moving the slider reveals records in their occurrence order. It does not assign them to an aftershock sequence or show when the records first became available.

Catalogues are revised. The values here come from the saved retrieval described in the methodology, even when you move back to an earlier event time.

Methodology & source documents

Follow the evidence all the way back.

The map uses a local equirectangular projection with geographic coordinates. Shading comes from a sampled agency grid; marker size identifies events and is not an energy or damage scale. Geographic labels are contextual. This feature makes no casualty or forecasting calculation.

Catalogue selection: Regional catalogue context. These four events are selected by place, date and magnitude; this does not establish an aftershock relationship. Occurrence time is separate from when a record became known. Window: Sep 1, 2026, 12:00:00 AM UTC to Sep 13, 2026, 12:00:00 AM UTC. Minimum magnitude 3; bounds 102–112°E and 10–2°S. Saved records are not automatically refreshed.
