Void and the Pursuit of Transformation

the void essential to living that we need to not fill

Jan 16, 2026 By Karina

I was watching this video online the other day, and this girl was talking about why it's so hard to move on from a relationship at first. She talked about how you miss the person and want to get back with them because you realize you now have a void in you and there is something missing, and you attribute that hole to them. But then the girl talked about how the hole is always there, you just now credit it to them and think that if you had them again the hole would be filled. But it wouldn’t be. It’s constant. This got me thinking about what drives us. I became more cognizant of this void in my own self and realized that it truly is always there. When we are with someone it distracts us from it, and when they are gone, so is that distraction. We’re no longer ignorant to the missing parts of us.

I think this void is really interesting to me because it made me realize that this little void we notice when we break up with someone is responsible for all human discovery. Everything is intertwined. That void within you is one that wants to be filled, but it never will be. But we don’t think that. It draws us to find things purposeful and fulfilling to us. And while we may be fulfilled momentarily, everything is temporary. This too shall pass. And I mean that in a positive way. If we had no void and were completely fulfilled, we would be totally stagnant. Totally static. We wouldn’t be moving forward, we wouldn’t be moving at all. This goes against everything we know. The only constant in life is change. That is one thing that is for certain.

Before the discovery of atoms and advanced knowledge of materials, pre-Socratic philosophers spent time trying to figure out what the world is comprised of. Heraclitus, for example, believed the Earth was entirely made of fire, just in different applications of it. He thought this because he noticed that everything is always changing, just like fire. A flame is never stagnant until extinguished, when it fails to even exist. Without the void, we are extinguished. One of Heraclitus’s famous quotes is, "No man ever steps in the same river twice, for it's not the same river and he's not the same man." This is one of my favorite quotes because it really makes me understand that everything is changing. Everything is in a constant state of becoming, as he would say.

We are in a constant state of becoming. I am eighteen years old. I am not who I will be, I am becoming that person, though, and I always will. I am in pursuit of transformation always, we all are. The void that fills us, or rather, does the opposite, is what keeps us moving. I like to think of it as a magnet. We are pulled to more. Pulled to transformation. Pulled to purpose. Nobody should take that from you. These magnets are what drew Camus to explore meaning and define absurdism. What drew Zuckerberg to FaceBook. What drew Newton to understanding motion. What drew me to writing this.

The void you feel in a time of loneliness is always there. It’s not novel. You were just distracted. It’s not a bad thing. It’s necessary, it’s evolutionary. You cannot attribute your need or pursuit for purpose to a break up or a loss. Don’t ignore the void, don’t distract yourself. But don’t try to fill it, either. You can’t. That’s by the design though. It keeps you moving. Embrace that hole. You don’t want to be stagnant. No man ever steps in the same river twice, so don’t stop in the river. You’ll drown.
