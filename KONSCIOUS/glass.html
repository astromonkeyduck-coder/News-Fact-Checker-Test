Glass Shards & The Illusion of Regret

regret is a façade!!!

Jan 16, 2026 By Karina

I think regret is an illusion.

Earlier this year, we did an activity during our senior activity where we were paired up with a random person in our class, and we both had to answer what our biggest regret was. We both went silent. Then we answered and said we didn’t think we had one. I jokingly said maybe certain hairstyles, and my classmate agreed, but we both came to the conclusion that there was nothing we would go back and change.

This made me realize how grateful I was for every outcome that has come my way so far, whether it was joyous, heartbreaking, surprising, hurtful, awkward. Because I have grown from every single one, or at least, I have made an effort to. Because of this, there is nothing I regret having happened, or regret having done. Every time I have made a mistake recently, I do not get mad, I acknowledge that I would not like for it to happen again and learn what I need to do to prevent that from happening again. I think some lessons require multiple mistakes to learn through, though, and that is especially prevalent in high school.

I would like to preface that I see regret as separate from remorse. I truly feel deep remorse for any person’s feelings I have hurt when making mistakes. I obviously feel remorse for upsetting or inconveniencing anyone, and would not want that to happen. However, I feel like reflecting upon the actions I took that might have hurt someone is imperative to growth. Especially during the key years of adolescence, like high school. I am beyond grateful to have gone through the events I have throughout high school, because I feel they have better helped me learn to navigate life as I go on to being a young adult. I look back at past relationships that were actually insane… (like actually) but I don’t regret them. They taught me so much, and still continue to teach me about how to interact with others, create boundaries, and move on. When I broke up with my first boyfriend, the last text I sent him was “thank you for the learning experience,” and I still stand by that. One of my favorite videos is an interview with talk-show host Stephen Colbert where he is reflecting on one of his dearest phrases: “what punishments of God are not gifts?” He talks about learning to love what he most wished hadn’t happened. Watching him speak so eloquently about this topic that is obviously so dear to him really impacted me. I reflected on things that I felt had held me back in the past before. I thought about my deep regret in getting poor grades junior year, and immediately diminished that regret I was holding. I looked at all the events that happened junior year that affected my performance, and I wrote about how they bettered my life. While they didn’t improve it in the short-term, they improved it long-term. Things that felt like hell to live through have sprouted beautiful flowers in my current life. I learned about what truly matters to me, what it means to be present, how to prioritize yourself, and how to have an uplifting mindset from events that in the moment taught me the opposite of that. This isn’t magically done, though, you have to choose to get better. You have to choose to look for the good. For example, if you go through a friendship breakup with someone so close to you, you have to understand what happened and want to learn from it. You want to know what you contributed, and you have to be okay with accepting being wrong. You cannot look back with spite, and think “I was in the right.” You have to look back as if you are grading an assignment, you look for what you should have done differently. What the friendship breakup means to you: maybe you had different values, needed to set up boundaries, or didn’t have enough time for them or they didn’t have it for you. Looking back at this allows you to better understand what is most important for you. If the conflict was a result of a breach of morals, you learn what values are most important to you and non-negotiable in future friendships. I think every hurtful or impactful event is like a piece of glass. You can continue looking at how sharp the shard is, and how it makes you bleed, or you can hold up the glass like a magnifying lens, and understand the past to learn from it.

Everything that has happened has made me who I am. And as of late, I like who I am. I like looking at things positively and trying to grow from them. I want to evolve, not stay stagnant. The past is fuel that allows me to keep going. If I got my way and wished things didn’t happen, I wouldn’t be able to grow.

Every “regretful” event is an opportunity to gain a tool that helps you in life, and I want a whole toolbox. Maybe not a drill, though, those scare me.
