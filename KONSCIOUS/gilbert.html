Gilbert & Giving In to the Comfort of Sadness

Jan 16, 2026 By Karina

I watched a video about the desire to be sad a few months ago. I honestly don’t remember what the video all said, but the title resonated with me. As a kid, I used to be super happy and full of light. I still think I am that way today, but I feel that light has dimmed. There are waves of ups and downs, and during the ups, I make healthy changes to remain there. Every up I feel like this is it. This is when I get to be happy forever like how I was as a child. But then this feeling creeps in: discomfort. I’m not used to being so foolishly happy, it’s uncomfortable to me. Then I realize that’s the desire to be sad. That’s such a crazy thing to think about– I would never want to intentionally be sad, but I think it’s because being happy is difficult, and being sad is the easier choice. It’s comfortable, it’s convenient, and that’s what makes it desirable. So after every up, comes a down. One that seems to dive deeper each time. This time, though, I want to stay on this up. The weather is nicer. The people are kinder. The food is better. It feels uncomfortable, but that’s just the hurdle to overcome. “Nothing worth having ever comes easy,” says Theodore Roosevelt. And this is worth having.

I realize now that I have spent most of my high school life see-sawing between high highs and low lows. I don’t want to see-saw anymore, I want to beat the desire for what is comfortable, convenient, for what is sadness. I think I have the emotional maturity now to maintain this joy; in fact, I know I do. I am determined not to give in to what is easy, like I have always done before, and I feel more equipped than ever to persevere. This is my first real up as an adult, and I’m determined to make it last. “Be as you wish to seem,” says Socrates, and that’s exactly what I plan to do and will continue to do. I am starting to approach each day with the same toothy grin I had as a kid, this time without the baby teeth. I also know that everything shall pass, the phases of life, of being up up and down down, but I want the phases to transition smoother and with less discrepancy. I want to be that little kid full of life with that delusional optimism. And though the desire for sadness and comfort can be hard to fight against, I am willing to fight it for her.

“What caused this up?”, you might ask. To be honest, and a little crazy, I think it was a stuffed animal. When I was little, I was obsessed with plushies. It brought me so much joy playing with them and spending time with them. I remember the whimsical worlds and storylines I created for them, and the companionship I created with them and with my friends and their plushies. The other day, our school had a little holiday market. I saw the cutest little hamster plushie, and my friend saw the cutest billy goat plushie. I was filled with such an overwhelming amount of innocent joy when we got to buy them and take them with us. In the midst of a stressful season of college applications, decisions, exams, relationships, and commitments, I forgot what a gift it was to be able to live. To experience. To enjoy the little things in life that as a child seemed like the big things. This stuffed animal, as silly as it might seem, was a reminder that the delusional joys of childhood are not a thing of the past, they are attainable with the right perspective. Plus, another stuffed animal doesn’t hurt. Especially if it’s a chubby hamster named Gilbert.
