## Top 100 Must-Know Dates/Events

Organized chronologically. Know these cold.

1. **1453** - Fall of Constantinople: ends Byzantine Empire, accelerates Italian Renaissance as Greek scholars migrate west
2. **1455** - Gutenberg Bible printed: movable-type printing press revolutionizes communication

5. **1488** - Dias rounds Cape of Good Hope: opens sea route toward Indian Ocean

37. **1682** - Louis XIV moves court to Versailles: symbol of French absolutism

39. **1687** - Newton publishes *Principia Mathematica*: crowning achievement of the Scientific Revolution

43. **1700--1721** - Great Northern War: Russia replaces Sweden as dominant Baltic power
44. **1707** - Act of Union: England and Scotland form Great Britain
45. **1713** - Treaty of Utrecht: ends War of Spanish Succession; balance-of-power principle
46. **1733** - John Kay's flying shuttle: early step in textile mechanization
47. **1740--1748** - War of Austrian Succession: Maria Theresa defends Habsburg inheritance

52. **1764** - Beccaria publishes *On Crimes and Punishments*: Enlightenment penal reform
53. **1769** - Watt patents improved steam engine: accelerates Industrial Revolution

56. **1789** - French Revolution begins: storming of the Bastille (July 14); Declaration of the Rights of Man (August 26)

58. **1792** - French Republic declared; War of the First Coalition begins

64. **1812** - Napoleon invades Russia: catastrophic retreat; beginning of the end
65. **1814--1815** - Congress of Vienna: restores conservative order; Concert of Europe; balance of power

67. **1830** - July Revolution (France): Charles X replaced by Louis-Philippe; Belgian independence

75. **1862--1871** - German unification under Bismarck: wars with Denmark, Austria, France; Empire proclaimed at Versailles (1871)

77. **1871** - German Empire proclaimed: shifts European balance of power
78. **1884--1885** - Berlin Conference: European powers partition Africa (Scramble for Africa)
79. **1894--1906** - Dreyfus Affair (France): exposes anti-Semitism; divides French society
80. **1905** - Russian Revolution of 1905: Bloody Sunday; Tsar concedes Duma; October Manifesto
81. **1905** - Einstein's special relativity and *annus mirabilis* papers: new physics
82. **1914** - Assassination of Archduke Franz Ferdinand: triggers World War I (alliance system, militarism, nationalism)
83. **1917** - Russian Revolutions: February (Tsar abdicates); October (Bolsheviks seize power under Lenin)
84. **1918** - Armistice ends World War I (November 11); Wilson's Fourteen Points
85. **1919** - Treaty of Versailles: war guilt, reparations, League of Nations, redrawn borders
86. **1922** - Mussolini's March on Rome: fascism takes power in Italy
87. **1929** - Wall Street Crash / onset of Great Depression: economic crisis destabilizes democracies
88. **1933** - Hitler becomes Chancellor of Germany: Nazi consolidation of power; Reichstag Fire; Enabling Act
89. **1936--1939** - Spanish Civil War: testing ground for fascism and communism
90. **1938** - Munich Agreement: appeasement; Kristallnacht
91. **1939** - Nazi-Soviet Pact; Germany invades Poland: World War II begins
92. **1941--1945** - Holocaust: systematic genocide of 6 million Jews and millions of others
93. **1945** - End of World War II in Europe (V-E Day, May 8): Yalta and Potsdam conferences

95. **1948** - Berlin Blockade and Airlift: first major Cold War crisis
96. **1957** - Treaty of Rome: establishes EEC (European Economic Community); beginning of European integration
97. **1961** - Berlin Wall constructed: physical symbol of Cold War division
98. **1989** - Fall of the Berlin Wall: revolutions across Eastern Europe end communist rule
99. **1991** - Dissolution of the Soviet Union: Cold War ends; new states emerge

## Top 50 Periodization Anchors

These dates mark turning points that divide one era from another. Know *why* each is a boundary.

1. **c. 1450 - Start of AP Euro scope** - Renaissance in full swing; printing press; centralization of states; transition from medieval to early modern.
2. **1453 - Fall of Constantinople** - End of the Eastern Roman Empire; Greek scholars flee west; Ottoman control of eastern Mediterranean redirects trade and scholarship.
3. **1455 - Gutenberg's printing press** - Transforms information distribution; enables Reformation, Scientific Revolution, and Enlightenment.
4. **1492 - Columbus reaches the Americas** - Opens Age of Exploration; Columbian Exchange reshapes global economies and demographics.
5. **1517 - Luther's 95 Theses** - Divides Western Christendom; triggers a century of religious conflict; challenges papal authority permanently.
6. **1521 - Diet of Worms** - Luther's formal break with Rome crystallizes the Reformation as a political as well as theological movement.
7. **1543 - Copernicus publishes *On the Revolutions*** - Beginning of the Scientific Revolution; new epistemology challenges Church authority.
8. **1545--1563 - Council of Trent** - Defines the Catholic Counter-Reformation; Church adapts rather than collapses.
9. **1555 - Peace of Augsburg** - First legal recognition of religious division in the Holy Roman Empire; but excludes Calvinists, ensuring future conflict.
10. **1588 - Defeat of the Spanish Armada** - Marks Spain's slow decline and England/Netherlands' rise as maritime and commercial powers.
11. **1598 - Edict of Nantes** - Ends French Wars of Religion; model of limited toleration.
12. **1618 - Start of the Thirty Years' War** - Last great European war of religion; devastation transforms the political order.
13. **1648 - Peace of Westphalia** - Establishes sovereign-state system; ends religious wars as a primary driver of interstate conflict.
14. **1649 - Execution of Charles I** - Demonstrates that monarchs can be held accountable; English experiment with republicanism.
15. **1685 - Revocation of the Edict of Nantes** - Represents the peak of Louis XIV's absolutism and religious uniformity.
16. **1687 - Newton's *Principia*** - Culmination of the Scientific Revolution; mechanistic universe becomes the dominant intellectual framework.
17. **1688--1689 - Glorious Revolution** - Establishes constitutional monarchy in England; becomes a model contrasted with continental absolutism.
18. **1690 - Locke's *Two Treatises*** - Philosophical foundation for Enlightenment political thought: natural rights, government by consent.
19. **1713 - Treaty of Utrecht** - Codifies balance of power as a diplomatic principle; ends French bid for European hegemony.
20. **1740s--1750s - Height of the Enlightenment** - *Encyclopedie*, Montesquieu, Voltaire, Rousseau reshape European intellectual culture.
21. **1756--1763 - Seven Years' War** - First truly global conflict; shifts colonial power to Britain; financial strain contributes to French Revolution.
22. **c. 1760s--1780s - Beginning of the Industrial Revolution** - Starts in Britain; mechanization transforms economy, society, and class structure.
23. **1776 - Smith's *Wealth of Nations*** - Intellectual foundation of free-market capitalism; challenges mercantilism.
24. **1789 - French Revolution begins** - Ends the Old Regime; popular sovereignty, citizenship, and rights enter European political vocabulary.
25. **1793--1794 - Reign of Terror** - Reveals radical potential of revolution; reaction against it shapes conservative thought.
26. **1799 - Napoleon's coup** - Military dictatorship replaces revolutionary republic; begins Napoleonic era.
27. **1804 - Napoleonic Code** - Standardizes civil law, abolishes feudal privileges, spreads revolutionary legal principles across Europe.
28. **1814--1815 - Congress of Vienna** - Conservative restoration; Concert of Europe manages great-power relations for a generation.
29. **1830 - July Revolution (France)** - Liberal monarchy replaces Bourbon restoration; signals that the Vienna settlement is cracking.
30. **1832 - Great Reform Act (Britain)** - Begins incremental democratization; middle class enters political system without revolution.
31. **1848 - Revolutions of 1848** - Liberal and nationalist movements erupt and fail; marks end of Metternich era; shift toward Realpolitik.
32. **1859--1871 - Italian and German unification** - Nationalism remakes the map of Europe; balance of power shifts.
33. **1861 - Emancipation of Russian serfs** - Russia begins painful modernization from above; foreshadows later instability.
34. **1870--1871 - Franco-Prussian War / German Empire** - New German Empire is the strongest continental power; France humiliated; alliance system begins.
35. **1884--1885 - Berlin Conference** - Formalizes the Scramble for Africa; high-water mark of European imperialism.
36. **1905 - Revolution of 1905 (Russia)** - Dress rehearsal for 1917; exposes fragility of tsarist autocracy.
37. **1914 - Outbreak of World War I** - Ends the long 19th century; total war, mass death, collapse of empires.
38. **1917 - Russian Revolutions** - First successful Marxist revolution; creates an ideological rival to liberal democracy for the rest of the century.
39. **1919 - Treaty of Versailles** - Redraws European borders; creates resentments that fuel interwar instability.
40. **1922 - Mussolini takes power** - First fascist regime; model for authoritarian movements across Europe.
41. **1929 - Great Depression begins** - Economic crisis discredits liberal democracy in many countries; empowers extremists.
42. **1933 - Hitler becomes Chancellor** - Nazism takes power legally; path to dictatorship, war, and genocide.
43. **1939 - World War II begins** - Most destructive conflict in human history; ends European global dominance.
44. **1945 - End of WWII in Europe** - Continent devastated; Cold War division begins; decolonization accelerates.
45. **1947 - Marshall Plan / Truman Doctrine** - U.S. commits to rebuilding and defending Western Europe; Cold War solidifies.
46. **1957 - Treaty of Rome (EEC)** - European integration begins; economic cooperation as path to lasting peace.
47. **1961 - Berlin Wall built** - Physical embodiment of Cold War division; Iron Curtain made literal.
48. **1989 - Fall of the Berlin Wall** - Revolutions of 1989 end communist rule in Eastern Europe; Cold War order collapses.
49. **1991 - Dissolution of the Soviet Union** - End of the Cold War; unipolar moment; new European order.
50. **1993 - Maastricht Treaty (EU)** - European Union created; deepened integration (single currency, common foreign policy); new questions about sovereignty and identity.

## Top 50 Comparison Pairs

Each pair is a natural AP exam comparison (think LEQ and SAQ prompts).

1. Italian Renaissance vs. Northern Renaissance - secular humanism and classical aesthetics vs. Christian humanism and social reform
2. Machiavelli vs. Erasmus - cynical realism about power vs. optimistic humanism about moral reform
3. Luther vs. Calvin - justification by faith alone and princely alliance vs. predestination and theocratic community (Geneva)
4. Protestant Reformation vs. Catholic Counter-Reformation - challenge to Church authority vs. internal reform and reassertion of doctrine
5. Henry VIII's Reformation vs. Luther's Reformation - political/dynastic motives vs. theological conviction
6. Spanish Empire vs. Dutch Republic (16th--17th c.) - Catholic absolutism and bullion wealth vs. commercial republic and religious toleration
7. Philip II (Spain) vs. Elizabeth I (England) - Catholic champion and overextension vs. Protestant pragmatist and naval power
8. French Wars of Religion vs. Thirty Years' War - national religious civil war (1562--1598) vs. international religious/political war (1618--1648)
9. Peace of Augsburg (1555) vs. Peace of Westphalia (1648) - limited religious settlement (Lutheran/Catholic only) vs. broader sovereignty principle (adds Calvinism)
10. Louis XIV (French absolutism) vs. William III (English constitutionalism) - divine-right centralization vs. parliamentary monarchy
11. English Civil War vs. French Revolution - 17th-c. constitutional struggle vs. 18th-c. radical social transformation
12. Hobbes vs. Locke - absolute sovereignty from fear vs. limited government from natural rights
13. Versailles (Louis XIV) vs. Parliament (William III) - court culture as political control vs. legislative negotiation as governance
14. Mercantilism vs. Adam Smith's free-market economics - state-directed trade for national power vs. laissez-faire for wealth creation
15. Scientific Revolution vs. Enlightenment - new methods of understanding nature vs. application of reason to society, politics, and religion
16. Copernicus/Galileo vs. Newton - revolutionary hypotheses vs. grand synthesis (laws of motion, gravity)
17. Voltaire vs. Rousseau - rational skepticism, tolerance, and elitism vs. emotion, popular sovereignty, and general will
18. Enlightened absolutism (Frederick II, Catherine II, Joseph II) vs. traditional absolutism (Louis XIV, Philip II) - reform from above using Enlightenment ideas vs. divine-right centralization
19. Frederick the Great (Prussia) vs. Maria Theresa (Austria) - militarized efficiency vs. administrative reform of a multiethnic empire
20. British industrialization vs. continental industrialization - early, organic, private-led vs. later, state-supported, with catch-up dynamics
21. First Industrial Revolution (textiles, steam, iron) vs. Second Industrial Revolution (steel, chemicals, electricity, oil)
22. French Revolution's liberal phase (1789--1792) vs. radical phase (1792--1794) - constitutional monarchy and rights vs. republic, Terror, and dechristianization
23. Jacobins vs. Girondins - radical centralization and Terror vs. moderate federalism
24. Napoleon as revolutionary vs. Napoleon as authoritarian - Code Napoleon, meritocracy, abolition of feudalism vs. censorship, secret police, dynasty
25. Congress of Vienna (1814--1815) vs. Treaty of Versailles (1919) - conservative restoration and balance of power vs. national self-determination and punitive terms
26. Metternich's conservatism vs. Mazzini's nationalism - defend the old order and suppress revolution vs. liberal nationalism as a liberating force
27. 1830 Revolutions vs. 1848 Revolutions - limited liberal gains (France, Belgium) vs. broader but ultimately failed liberal-nationalist uprisings
28. Cavour (Italian unification) vs. Bismarck (German unification) - diplomatic realism and Piedmont-led leadership vs. blood-and-iron Prussian militarism
29. Garibaldi vs. Cavour - romantic revolutionary nationalism from below vs. calculating diplomatic statecraft from above
30. Bismarck's diplomacy (1871--1890) vs. Wilhelm II's Weltpolitik (1890--1914) - careful alliance management vs. aggressive imperialism and naval race
31. British imperialism vs. French imperialism - indirect rule and commercial empire vs. mission civilisatrice and assimilation ideology
32. Social Darwinism vs. Marxism - competition and racial hierarchy justify inequality vs. class struggle and revolution to overthrow capitalism
33. Marx vs. Bernstein (revisionism) - revolutionary overthrow vs. evolutionary socialism through democratic participation
34. Causes of WWI: alliance system vs. nationalism - structural diplomatic entanglement vs. ideological fervor and ethnic rivalry
35. Western Front (WWI) vs. Eastern Front (WWI) - trench stalemate and attrition vs. more mobile warfare and imperial collapse
36. Wilson's Fourteen Points vs. Clemenceau's demands at Versailles - idealism and self-determination vs. security and punishment for Germany
37. Weimar Republic vs. Third Republic (France) - fragile democracy destroyed by extremism vs. durable democracy surviving crises (Dreyfus, WWI)
38. Fascism (Italy) vs. Nazism (Germany) - authoritarian nationalism without systematic racial genocide vs. totalitarian racial state and Holocaust
39. Stalin's USSR vs. Hitler's Germany - communist totalitarianism (collectivization, purges) vs. fascist totalitarianism (racial ideology, conquest)
40. Appeasement (Chamberlain) vs. containment (Truman) - concession to avoid war vs. resistance to prevent expansion
41. Marshall Plan (Western Europe) vs. Soviet control of Eastern Europe - economic aid fostering recovery and integration vs. command economies and political repression
42. NATO vs. Warsaw Pact - Western collective defense vs. Soviet-dominated military alliance
43. Decolonization: British withdrawal (India, Africa) vs. French withdrawal (Algeria, Indochina) - relatively negotiated transitions vs. protracted wars
44. De Gaulle's France vs. Adenauer's West Germany - independent nationalism within the West vs. integration and rehabilitation through European institutions
45. Keynesianism (postwar welfare states) vs. Thatcherism/neoliberalism (1980s) - state management of demand and social provision vs. deregulation, privatization, and austerity
46. Solidarity (Poland) vs. Prague Spring (Czechoslovakia) - successful grassroots challenge to communism (1980s) vs. crushed reform (1968)
47. Gorbachev's reforms (glasnost/perestroika) vs. Brezhnev Doctrine - opening and restructuring vs. military enforcement of communist orthodoxy
48. Revolutions of 1989 (mostly peaceful) vs. Revolutions of 1848 (mostly failed) - successful democratic transitions ending Cold War vs. failed liberal-nationalist uprisings
49. European integration (EU expansion) vs. rising nationalism (Brexit, populism) - supranational cooperation vs. sovereignty and identity backlash
50. Cold War bipolarity vs. post-1991 European order - two-bloc division vs. EU expansion, NATO enlargement, and new security challenges

## Top 50 Causation Chains

Each chain shows how one development leads to the next. Practice explaining the *mechanisms* connecting each link.

1. Fall of Constantinople (1453) --> Greek scholars flee to Italy --> revival of classical texts --> Italian Renaissance flourishes
2. Gutenberg's printing press (1455) --> rapid spread of printed books --> rising literacy --> enables Reformation and Scientific Revolution
3. Portuguese navigation technology --> exploration of African coast --> direct sea route to Asia (da Gama, 1498) --> bypasses Ottoman/Venetian trade monopoly
4. Columbus reaches Americas (1492) --> Columbian Exchange --> influx of silver and new crops --> price revolution and demographic transformation in Europe
5. Spanish silver from Americas --> price revolution (inflation) --> declining real wages --> social unrest and economic shift toward Atlantic economies
6. Renaissance humanism --> critical study of biblical texts --> Erasmus's Greek New Testament --> Luther questions Church teaching
7. Printing press --> spread of Luther's 95 Theses (1517) --> Reformation --> Wars of Religion --> Peace of Westphalia (1648)
8. Luther's break with Rome --> princely adoption of Protestantism --> Peace of Augsburg (1555) --> religious division institutionalized in HRE
9. Calvin in Geneva --> spread of Calvinism to France, Netherlands, Scotland --> religious wars and resistance theory
10. Henry VIII's desire for annulment --> break with Rome (1534) --> English Reformation --> Tudor religious instability --> Elizabethan Settlement
11. Counter-Reformation (Jesuits, Council of Trent) --> Catholic revival --> reconversion of parts of Central Europe --> intensified religious conflict
12. St. Bartholomew's Day Massacre (1572) --> radicalization of Huguenots --> prolonged French Wars of Religion --> Edict of Nantes (1598)
13. Dutch Revolt against Spain --> independence of Dutch Republic --> commercial golden age --> Amsterdam as financial center of Europe
14. Copernicus (1543) --> Galileo's observations --> conflict with Church --> Newton's synthesis (1687) --> mechanistic worldview
15. Scientific Revolution --> empiricism and rational inquiry --> Enlightenment --> questioning of traditional authority (Church, monarchy)
16. Thirty Years' War devastation --> Peace of Westphalia --> sovereign state system --> decline of HRE --> rise of France
17. Louis XIV's absolutism --> centralization + wars --> financial exhaustion of France --> fiscal crisis inherited by Louis XVI
18. Revocation of Edict of Nantes (1685) --> Huguenot exodus --> loss of skilled artisans/merchants --> weakens French economy, strengthens rivals
19. Glorious Revolution (1688) --> Bill of Rights, constitutional monarchy --> model for Enlightenment thinkers (Locke, Montesquieu, Voltaire)
20. Bank of England (1694) --> public credit and national debt system --> British ability to finance wars --> commercial and naval supremacy
21. Enclosure movement --> displaced rural workers --> available labor force --> factory system and urbanization (Industrial Revolution)
22. Enlightenment ideas (Locke, Rousseau, Montesquieu) --> challenge to divine right and privilege --> ideological basis for American and French Revolutions
23. Seven Years' War (1756--1763) --> British colonial dominance but massive debt --> taxation of American colonies --> American Revolution --> inspiration for French Revolution
24. Financial crisis of French crown --> calling of Estates-General (1789) --> Third Estate forms National Assembly --> French Revolution begins
25. Fall of the Bastille (1789) --> Declaration of Rights of Man --> constitutional monarchy --> radicalization --> Republic (1792) --> Terror (1793--1794)
26. Reign of Terror --> Thermidorian Reaction --> Directory (weak government) --> Napoleon's coup (1799) --> authoritarian stability
27. Napoleonic conquests --> spread of Code Napoleon and nationalist ideas --> resistance movements --> post-1815 nationalist aspirations
28. Napoleon's invasion of Spain --> Spanish colonial instability --> Latin American independence movements
29. Congress of Vienna (1815) --> conservative order --> suppression of liberalism/nationalism --> resentment builds --> Revolutions of 1830 and 1848
30. Industrialization in Britain --> urbanization, factory system --> new working class --> labor movements, Chartism, socialism, Marxism
31. Industrialization --> demand for raw materials and markets --> economic imperialism --> Scramble for Africa and Asia
32. Railroad expansion --> faster transport of goods and troops --> national market integration --> support for political unification (Germany, Italy)
33. Failure of 1848 Revolutions --> shift from romantic nationalism to Realpolitik --> Cavour and Bismarck use war and diplomacy to unify Italy and Germany
34. Bismarck's wars of unification (1864, 1866, 1870--1871) --> German Empire --> shifts balance of power --> French desire for revenge (revanchism)
35. Franco-Prussian War (1870--1871) --> French humiliation and loss of Alsace-Lorraine --> revanchism --> contributes to alliance system before WWI
36. Bismarck's alliance system (Triple Alliance) --> German isolation after his dismissal (1890) --> Franco-Russian Alliance --> rigid alliance blocs
37. Industrialization + nationalism + imperial rivalry + alliance system --> arms race --> assassination of Franz Ferdinand (1914) --> World War I
38. WWI trench warfare and total war --> massive casualties and economic strain --> disillusionment --> collapse of Russian, German, Austro-Hungarian, Ottoman empires
39. WWI strain on Russia --> food shortages, military defeats --> February Revolution (1917) --> Provisional Government --> October Revolution --> Bolshevik regime
40. Treaty of Versailles (1919) --> German resentment (war guilt, reparations, lost territory) --> Weimar instability --> rise of Hitler

42. Hitler's expansionism --> appeasement (Munich, 1938) --> emboldened aggression --> invasion of Poland (1939) --> World War II
43. WWII --> devastation of Europe --> power vacuum --> U.S. and USSR emerge as superpowers --> Cold War
44. Holocaust --> moral reckoning --> Universal Declaration of Human Rights (1948) --> creation of Israel --> commitment to "never again"
45. Marshall Plan (1947) --> Western European economic recovery --> political stability --> foundation for European integration (EEC, 1957)
